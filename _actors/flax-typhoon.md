## Who they are

**Flax Typhoon** (Microsoft's name; also tracked as **Ethereal Panda** by CrowdStrike and overlapping with activity reported as **Red Juliett**) is a China-nexus, state-sponsored espionage actor active since at least mid-2021. US and allied authorities tie the group's infrastructure and tooling to **Integrity Technology Group**, a Beijing-based company the US Treasury has sanctioned for supporting PRC government cyber operations. On 2026-10-08, the US Department of Justice and FBI announced the seizure of seven domains behind two Integrity Tech-built tools - **MicroScan**, a vulnerability scanner, and **FishHub**, a spear-phishing platform - that the group used to identify and breach victim networks. The action was paired with a joint advisory (AA26-281A) co-authored by the FBI, CISA, and NSA alongside agencies from the UK, Australia, Canada, Japan, New Zealand, and Spain, and CISA added five of the vulnerabilities the group exploited to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, with a federal patch-or-disconnect deadline of 2026-10-11. This is the second major US disruption of Integrity Tech's infrastructure - the first dismantled a Flax Typhoon-linked Mirai-variant IoT botnet of more than 200,000 devices in September 2024.

## Behavioral pattern

- **Vulnerability-scanning-as-a-service.** MicroScan was used to mass-scan for exploitable public-facing systems, with documented targets including a South Carolina power company, airports in Japan and Poland, Taiwanese natural-gas and power utilities, a multinational NGO, and Taiwanese universities - a spread spanning energy, transportation, and research-sector critical infrastructure.
- **Old, unglamorous CVEs as the way in.** The advisory ties the group to exploitation of at least eight vulnerabilities, five of which CISA added to its KEV catalog: a decade-old ProFTPD arbitrary file read/write flaw (CVE-2015-3306, CVSS 10.0), an ONLYOFFICE Docs path-traversal-to-RCE bug (CVE-2021-3199), a Strapi cleartext-credential-storage flaw (CVE-2023-22894), an Apache Struts Dynamic-Method-Invocation command injection (CVE-2016-3081), and an ISC BIND reachable-assertion DoS (CVE-2015-5477). None of these are novel - they're long-patched bugs in software many organizations never fully retire, which is exactly what makes them durable initial-access paths for a patient actor.
- **Spear-phishing as a parallel access route.** FishHub gave the group a second initial-access lane independent of unpatched infrastructure, generating phishing lures that delivered remote access directly into victim networks.
- **Primary focus on Taiwan, with secondary reach elsewhere.** Microsoft's original 2023 reporting described the group maintaining long-term access to Taiwanese government, education, critical-manufacturing, and IT-sector networks with minimal malware footprint, alongside smaller clusters of activity in Southeast Asia, North America, and Africa.
- **Living-off-the-land over custom malware.** Once inside, the group has been consistently observed renaming a legitimate SoftEther VPN client to mimic benign Windows system binaries (e.g. `conhost.exe`), fetched via `Invoke-WebRequest`, to establish a persistent VPN-over-HTTPS bridge back into the environment. Lateral movement and collection lean on built-in tooling - WMIC, WinRM, Mimikatz, and the Juicy Potato privilege-escalation tool - rather than custom implants, and web shells (China Chopper, GodZilla) are used for initial persistence on compromised public-facing servers.
- **Deep, patient dwell.** Victims in the 2023 Microsoft disclosure showed multi-year undetected presence, consistent with an intelligence-collection mission rather than financially motivated crime.

## What this means for defenders

Flax Typhoon's current campaign is a reminder that **unpatched legacy software is still a first-class initial-access vector for state actors**, not just commodity crimeware - the five KEV-listed CVEs here date as far back as 2015, and the group's own scanning tool exists specifically to find organizations that never finished retiring that software. Because the group avoids custom malware once inside, process-level signature detection largely misses it; the more durable signal is behavioral - a SoftEther VPN executable fetched via PowerShell and renamed to impersonate a system binary, then establishing outbound HTTPS to non-corporate infrastructure, is a pattern that survives changes in which specific CVE got the group in the door.
