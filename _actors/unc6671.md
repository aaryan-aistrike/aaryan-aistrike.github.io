## Who they are

UNC6671 is a financially motivated extortion group tracked by Google's Threat Intelligence Group (GTIG) and Mandiant. It operated publicly under the "BlackFile" brand from early 2025 until announcing a (false) shutdown on 2026-05-11 - then immediately continued the same core intrusion activity under four new public-facing extortion brands: **Redact, Pink, Helix, and Falcon**. GTIG assesses a single core intrusion group drives the vishing and cloud data-theft campaigns behind all five brand names, based on shared phishing infrastructure linking them together. Between January and May 2026 alone, tracked ransom payments to the group totaled roughly 141.65 BTC (~$10.69M USD), with negotiated final payments averaging around $750,000 against initial demands of $1M-$3M.

## Behavioral pattern

- **Helpdesk vishing on personal devices.** Operators call enterprise employees directly on their personal mobile phones - not work lines - impersonating internal IT helpdesk staff and citing an urgent, mandatory security migration, FIDO2 passkey enrollment, or MFA update.
- **Adversary-in-the-middle credential harvesting.** Victims are walked through a spoofed login portal on a lookalike domain (e.g. `[company].createssopasskey[.]com`, `[company].addssopasskey[.]com`, `passkeyhelpdesk[.]com`) that proxies the real login flow in real time, intercepting both password and MFA/passkey response.
- **Rapid, escalating domain-registration tempo.** Root-domain registration accelerated from roughly one every 2.2 days (April-May 2026) to one every 1.6 days (June-July 2026), with a peak of 7 new domains in a 72-hour window in late July - indicative of an actively scaling, well-resourced operation rather than a one-off campaign.
- **Cloud-native data exfiltration.** Post-compromise, automated scripts (built on `python-requests`, PowerShell, and Go HTTP clients) pull data directly out of Microsoft 365, Okta, and other SaaS platforms - no malware drop or endpoint foothold required.
- **Self-cleanup of security alerts.** Operators delete password-reset confirmation emails and MFA-configuration-change notifications from compromised mailboxes, and initiate further unauthorized password resets, to buy time before the victim organization notices.
- **Deliberate sector pivot toward high-value targets.** Initial targeting (April-May 2026) spanned manufacturing, real estate, healthcare, and insurance. By July 2026 the group had pivoted specifically to private equity firms, hedge funds, major law firms, and financial rating agencies - organizations that sit on M&A, capital deployment, and litigation data with outsized extortion leverage.
- **No confirmed nation-state attribution.** Infrastructure is hosted across Cloudflare, DDOS-GUARD, and Private Layer, with residential proxies sourced from US ISPs (AT&T, Comcast, Charter). GTIG assesses this as financially motivated cybercrime rather than state-sponsored activity.

## What this means for defenders

UNC6671's entire initial-access chain runs through a channel most security stacks don't monitor at all: a phone call to a personal device, followed by a browser session on an employee's own device to a domain the company never registered. Neither of those touches corporate email security or EDR. The defensible control point is the **MFA/passkey re-enrollment event itself** - any unscheduled "register new security info" or passkey-enrollment event in the identity provider (Entra ID, Okta) is either legitimate self-service or an active AiTM compromise in progress, and it's worth alerting on as a sequence with any subsequent mail-rule creation or bulk SaaS data access from that account. Employee-facing helpdesk-impersonation awareness training needs to explicitly cover "IT will never call your personal phone about an urgent passkey/MFA migration" as a standing rule, not a one-time reminder.
