## Who they are

UAT-11587 is a China-nexus cyberespionage actor tracked by Cisco Talos, first observed in September 2025 during a spear-phishing campaign against Taiwan's academic, think-tank, and civil-society policy community. Over the roughly eleven months that followed (through July 2026), Talos' incident responders identified at least 16 institutional environments either affected or targeted across eight Asian countries - Taiwan, India, the Philippines, Cambodia, Pakistan, Thailand, Myanmar, and Syria - amounting to around 350 compromised endpoints. Talos assesses the China-nexus attribution with high confidence, based on multiple independent technical and operational indicators rather than any single artifact.

## Behavioral pattern

- **Decoy-document spear-phishing.** Initial access runs through spear-phishing emails carrying tailored decoy documents aimed at individuals inside government, policy, and civil-society organizations.
- **Living off Microsoft 365.** The group's custom backdoor, Antino, routes its entire command-and-control channel through the Microsoft Graph API, abusing legitimate Outlook and OneDrive functionality rather than standing up dedicated C2 infrastructure of its own.
- **Mailbox-as-dead-drop tasking.** Antino polls an attacker-controlled Outlook mailbox roughly every 10 seconds for messages prefixed `command_req_[session_id]`, executes the embedded instruction, and returns results via a matching `command_res_[session_id]` reply - and separately uploads host telemetry files to an OneDrive folder about once a minute.
- **Minimal, purpose-built tooling.** Antino is a Rust-compiled Windows backdoor, built in both 32-bit and 64-bit variants, offering host reconnaissance, shell/PowerShell execution, directory enumeration, file upload/download, in-memory shellcode loading, and straightforward Registry Run-key persistence - no more complexity than the mission needs.
- **High-value, narrow targeting.** Victims cluster in defense and national-security bodies, executive government agencies, diplomatic services, law enforcement, legislative institutions, government IT services, universities, think tanks, and civil-society/policy groups - consistent with an intelligence-collection mission rather than opportunistic or financially motivated activity.

## What this means for defenders

Because Antino's command-and-control traffic travels over `graph.microsoft.com` and `login.microsoftonline.com` - the same endpoints every legitimate Outlook/OneDrive client calls continuously - network allow-listing and domain-reputation tooling provide no coverage, and the C2 mailbox itself lives inside the attacker's own Microsoft 365 tenant, outside the victim organization's Entra ID audit boundary entirely. Detection has to shift to the endpoint: which specific process is making those calls, and whether that same process also just wrote a Registry Run-key. A non-Microsoft-signed binary doing both in close succession is a strong, protocol-agnostic signal regardless of which legitimate cloud service a future variant adopts as its dead drop.
