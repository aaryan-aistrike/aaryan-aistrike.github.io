## Who they are

UNC6508 is a PRC-nexus, espionage-motivated threat cluster that Google's Threat Intelligence Group (GTIG) attributed with high confidence to a multi-year campaign against North American academic, medical, and military research institutions, disclosed on 2026-06-15. The earliest known compromise - of a REDCap server belonging to a North American medical research institution - dates to September 2023, and GTIG observed continuing activity through at least November 2025, meaning the actor sat undetected inside at least one victim environment for more than two years. Victims span clinical providers, academic medical centers, military health institutions, professional advocacy groups, and health regulatory bodies across the US and Canada.

## Behavioral pattern

- **Legacy-version hunting.** UNC6508 targets externally facing REDCap (Research Electronic Data Capture) servers, specifically probing for older REDCap versions left installed side-by-side with current ones - a downgrade-attack opportunity GTIG could not fully trace back to a single disclosed CVE, but one REDCap itself has patched for in the past.
- **Deep application trojanization, not a bolt-on implant.** The group's custom malware, INFINITERED, doesn't drop a standalone binary - it injects itself across three legitimate REDCap application files: `Upgrade.php` (so the backdoor survives version upgrades), the authentication module (to harvest plaintext credentials from login POST requests), and the custom hooks file (a page-load-triggered backdoor that reads commands from a `REDCAP-TOKEN` cookie).
- **Patient, long-dwell tradecraft.** More than a year passed between initial compromise and the point where harvested credentials were used to reach a domain administrator account - a deliberately slow escalation built to stay below detection thresholds tuned for faster intrusions.
- **Novel abuse of a Workspace admin feature for exfiltration.** Once in possession of privileged Google Workspace credentials, the actor created a content compliance rule (internally named "Patroit") that silently BCC-forwarded any mail matching a keyword list - geostrategic policy terms, military-research subjects, and pathogen names like Chikungunya - to an actor-controlled Gmail account. GTIG describes this as a technique not previously observed from a PRC-nexus actor.
- **Intelligence-collection targeting.** Stated collection priorities included defense strategy, Indo-Pacific Command operations, AI and uncrewed vehicle systems, and specific medical research programs - consistent with a state-sponsored intelligence mission rather than financially motivated crime.
- **Layered operational security.** Access to victim environments and the exfiltration mailbox was routed through compromised routers, residential proxies, and VPS infrastructure (all presenting US-based IPs), and the exfiltration account itself was a disposable, mass-created Gmail address used for nothing else.

## What this means for defenders

INFINITERED lives entirely inside a web application rather than as an endpoint process, so standard EDR process-creation telemetry never sees it - the detectable surface is the REDCap application layer (unexpected changes to `Upgrade.php`, the auth module, or hooks files) and, more reliably, the cloud administration layer. Creating or modifying a Google Workspace content compliance rule that attaches a silent BCC or forwarding action is a rare, high-signal admin-console event in almost any organization, and it remains visible in Workspace audit logs regardless of how the attacker's credentials were originally obtained - making it a durable detection point even against a future actor using a completely different initial-access technique.
